### R1.3. Regression coefficient: standardized vs. unstandardized (p. 10, lines 40–44)

> - p.10 lines 40-44: changes the regression coefficient: standardized vs. unstandardized

**REPLY:**

We distinguished standardized from unstandardized regression coefficients and clarified NCA rescaling: *“In a regression model, multiplying a predictor by a positive constant changes its unstandardized slope but not its standardized slope or fitted predictions. Likewise, a positive affine transformation of either NCA axis changes the units of the reported bottleneck but leaves the normalized effect size $d = C/S$ unchanged, because the ceiling area and scope are rescaled by the same factor. The substantive concern is therefore not a change of units alone: nonlinear transformations, different ordinal thresholds, and different score-construction rules can change the geometry of the data cloud and, consequently, the estimated NCA effect and bottleneck interpretation.”*

### R1.7. Explain practical implementation (p. 26, lines 51–53)

> - p. 26 lines 51-53: easily said, but how is this supposed to work in practice? Some more explanation is warranted given the importance of this point

**REPLY:**

We specified a practical comparison with a correlational benchmark: *“Under association without necessity, significant NCA results became more frequent with stronger correlations and larger samples. Researchers should therefore compare an observed ceiling with a plausible correlational explanation before inferring necessity.”* *“One approach is to simulate data from a fitted correlational model using the study sample size and scoring procedure, then apply NCA to these datasets. The simulated effects provide a benchmark for the observed result. Robustness can also be checked across scoring methods and independent samples.”*
